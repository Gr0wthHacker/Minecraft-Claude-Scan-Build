# Premium refinement: additions around the existing park

## Direction

Make the park feel hosted, composed and inhabited. Keep the established colourful
roads, geometry, main structures and landscapes. Concentrate new detail where
guests arrive, choose, wait, watch and regroup.

**User constraint:** large amounts of pathway excavation or replacement are
unacceptable. This plan preserves the existing paving. An earlier isolated image
study explored quieter paving; that option is withdrawn from the implementation
plan. No source configuration or schematic was changed for that study.

The intended result is three carefully finished experiences:

- **Midway:** a festive pleasure garden, with welcoming entrances, attractive
  places to pause and a consistent family of timber, fabric and planting details.
- **Lost Plateau:** a believable expedition park. Field equipment, specimen
  interpretation and sheltered viewing belong beside the landscape and coaster.
- **Prismworks:** an extraordinary machine within dramatic terrain. Precise
  fixtures and clear observation points make its scale feel intentional.

Premium here means consistency and care at guest scale. A new object earns its
place by improving a view, comfort, orientation or the story of something nearby.

## What the current review shows

Evidence: the current `Park Complete`, the latest merged stitch captures, the
placement and decoration audits, and four newly rendered guest-level views.
The schematic has 440,951 blocks. The captures cover 536 of the 546 chunks
intersecting the park; unobserved edges are not treated as free space.

1. **The paths are already visually assertive.** Midway's ground-layer non-lawn
   surface is approximately 42% red/white wool and 30% dark border blocks.
   These figures describe the underlying ground layer, not a visibility-weighted
   count of the finished park. Keep that graphic language and repeat its colours
   selectively in nearby flags, awnings and signs, so the streets feel connected
   to their surroundings.
2. **The arrival already has a strong frame.** Its close-up experience can gain
   planting, well-placed information and finer construction details without
   another arch or anything on the central axis.
3. **Midway streets have substantial adjacent structures.** The useful additions
   are small and attached: brackets, hanging planting, coordinated fabric and
   thoughtfully arranged seating. Continuous extra structures would crowd them.
4. **The Plateau and Prism approach views are strongly enclosed by terrain.**
   Give these stretches a few meaningful details to encounter along the way,
   with open intervals. Keep the walking width and the final reveal clear.
5. **Basic decoration defects have been addressed.** The latest audit reports
   intact intended lamp masts, rooted trees, and no detected decoration blocking
   the tested paths or doors. The next pass should improve composition rather
   than increase the number of lamps or trees indiscriminately.
6. **Some guest-facing language still describes older operation.** For example,
   the Sky Lift frontage queue says “one car at a time.” Its operational wording
   needs to be reconciled with what guests can actually use. Older masterplans
   also describe retired attractions; they are not a current content checklist.

The renders simplify fences, leaves, slabs and fixtures. They support judgments
about massing, route composition and colour; fine-detail and night judgments
still require Minecraft views.

## The five strongest improvements

### 1. Finish the arrival as a welcome experience

**Area:** existing Welcome Court, V24–79 / U270–330, and the existing entrance.

Add two deliberately paired planting compositions in existing side beds:
a low planted foreground, one modest vertical element already supported by the
bed or façade, and a restrained warm light. Use the same forms on both sides,
with slight planting variation.

Mount a compact orientation board beside the walk where guests can stop without
holding up the entrance. Show the park's real destinations and return direction.
Give the existing gate and court structures a matching family of small brackets,
fascia edging and finished support bases where these fit outside the walkway.

**Guest outcome:** arriving feels like being welcomed into a place with a clear
identity. A first-time visitor can choose a direction immediately.

**Protection:** preserve the U300 entrance/court/wheel sightline, door approaches,
queue interfaces and fountain access. Additions use side beds and attachments;
the central walk receives no new object or ground overlay.

### 2. Turn two existing seating groups into inviting places to stay

**First area:** existing Midway Garden, V24–99 / U345–384. It already has repeated
seats, beds and pergolas; improve selected groups rather than adding another row.

Give one pair of seats a low planted backdrop, a small shared side table, and
an attached canopy detail or lantern on the existing pergola. Arrange the group
to look toward a garden or attraction, with a clear approach from the path.
Choose a second group with a different view so the two stops have distinct roles.

Add modest finishing around existing bed corners: a coherent cap, planting that
softens the edge, and a deliberate break at the entrance to the seating group.

**Guest outcome:** the garden has an obvious place for a group to regroup or
watch the park, beyond simply having chairs distributed along it.

**Protection:** retain every cross-path and accessible approach. Keep new tables
and planting inside the current seating/bed footprint. Confirm standing room in
front of the seats; do not assume Minecraft has a working sit interaction.

### 3. Give existing entrances a shared level of finish

**Areas:** the corrected avenue arches, Carousel entrance, Snack Window and
coaster porch. Treat one example in each land before extending the treatment.

Use a small, repeatable construction vocabulary:

- a finished footing contained within the current verge;
- a bracket or brace that visibly supports the fascia;
- a single consistent sign mount;
- a restrained planted or scenic element beside the approach;
- a light integrated with the existing structure where it is useful.

The style changes by land: warm timber and fabric in Midway, expedition timber
and rough stone in the Plateau, dark metal-like trim and pale light in Prismworks.
Repeat proportions and the quality of the construction, not an identical façade.

**Guest outcome:** attractions feel like parts of the same professionally
operated park, while each land keeps its character.

**Protection:** five recently realigned arches must remain aligned. Do not widen
their footings into paving, add extra free-standing gateway layers, or obscure
signs and boarding/exit routes.

### 4. Add small scenes which explain the place

Limit these to existing attractions and props, with one clear idea in each.

| Area | Addition | Guest-facing purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Coaster porch, around V25/U112 | A compact expedition dispatch vignette against an existing wall: a field crate, rolled equipment and one mounted route board | Makes the station feel prepared for an expedition; keeps the queue and boarding bay usable |
| Fossil Lab, around V130/U62 | One compact specimen interpretation display beside the existing approach | Connects the excavation to the lab; gives a spectator something specific to notice |
| Prism approach, U468 or U524 avenue | Finish an existing signal fixture with a mounting base, a small numbered plate and a related cable/bracket detail | Gives the equipment a reason to be there, tied to the Well |
| Existing Well rim viewpoint | A small interpretive plate and one carefully mounted light at an existing observation position | Helps a visitor understand what they are watching and where the return route is |

These are scenic additions, not new games, shops or promised interactions.
Do not scatter duplicate crates, barrels and boards through every empty patch.
Each scene needs a clear attachment to the attraction it explains.

### 5. Refine planting and lighting into compositions

Start with four small, high-visibility bed/entrance groups. Use a low front edge,
a middle layer and the existing taller planting behind. Keep each group to a
limited plant palette and repeat it locally. Most of the park's 351 trees stay
as they are; additional trees are not the default treatment.

For the two new Midway bunting spans, consider a small finishing pass: more
deliberate red/white pennants and capped supports, using their existing positions.
This would involve roughly ten small pennant substitutions rather than any path
work. Preserve their six-course clearance and avoid extending strings everywhere.

Use the existing lamp system as the foundation. Additional accent lights should
serve an entrance, sign or seating group, with a first-pass cap of eight. A night
walk must establish where more light is useful before adding more street masts.

**Guest outcome:** planting and light frame things worth looking at, and the
park contains both lively stretches and quieter places to pause.

## Supporting refinements

### A consistent information system

Separate three kinds of information: destinations at decisions, attraction names
at entrances, and instructions where an activity starts. Match sign mounts and
text hierarchy within each land. Mount new boards on existing structures where
possible to avoid additional posts.

Example short lines, all within the frontage generator's 15-character limit:

| Use | Example lines |
|---|---|
| Arrival | `WELCOME` / `CHOOSE YOUR WAY` |
| Plateau destination | `LOST PLATEAU` / `EXCAVATION WALK` |
| Garden | `THE GARDEN` / `REST A WHILE` |
| Well observation | `THE PRISM WELL` / `RIM GALLERY` |
| Proven return route | `RETURN LIFT` |

These are copy candidates, not automatic sign replacements. Arrows must follow
verified routes. Admission, ride, queue, payment and lift claims must reflect
actual server operation. A polished sign promising an unavailable experience
would undermine the intended result.

### Selective backstage screening

Where a verified guest view exposes storage or an unfinished service face, add
a short screen using the existing façade palette or planting. Keep staff doors,
maintenance access and emergency returns open. Do not screen the railway,
coaster mechanism or other intentionally visible engineering merely because it
looks technical. Select locations from guest views, not a uniform border rule.

### Smooth the joins with attachments

Add caps, corners, short end panels, supported brackets and planting collars
where independently built modules meet awkwardly. A small return panel or bed
edge can finish a junction without relocating either module. Any repair which
needs structural removal becomes a separate, explicitly scoped item.

## Work packages and placement requirements

The numbered map marks **review areas**, not final placement coordinates.
Every addition still needs a measured footprint and clear access in the current
assembly and latest observed world. A visually empty render is not permission
to place into a route, view corridor or service reservation.

| Package | Scope | Provisional added-block allowance | Removal expectation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A — welcome | Two side-bed compositions, one orientation board, modest gate trim | 120–220 | Zero paving; attachments only |
| B — garden hospitality | Improve two existing seating groups | 100–180 | Zero paving; use existing beds/pergolas |
| C — entrance finish | One prototype in each land | 120–220 | Zero paving; isolated fixture edits only if needed |
| D — scenic explanation | Three small scenes attached to current attractions | 150–260 | Zero paving; no new attraction footprint |
| E — planting/light/fabric | Four bed groups, existing bunting finish, selected accent lights | 100–180 | Zero paving; small fixture swaps listed separately |

These are design allowances, not generated bills of materials. The first set
totals approximately **590–1,060 added blocks**. Expansion should follow review,
not automatically consume a larger budget. Use the locked Skyblock 1.19 palette
and familiar spendable materials; costly materials are not required for this
direction. The old 265k masterplan budget needs reconciliation with the current
440,951-block assembly before treating it as available headroom.

**Recommended first build:** packages A and B, plus one small entrance-finish
prototype. This puts the first effort into places nearly every guest experiences
and demonstrates the intended quality before extending it to all three lands.

## How the guest journey should read

1. **Arrive:** a welcoming side composition and clear orientation, with an open
   view into the court.
2. **Choose:** the existing coloured routes lead toward recognisable land names.
3. **Approach:** a few related details establish the theme before the entrance.
4. **Participate or watch:** the entrance, instructions and nearby observation
   position agree about what the attraction offers.
5. **Regroup:** an attractive seating pocket is easy to reach without obstructing
   those continuing along the route.
6. **Return:** directions lead to the hub or onward destination without passing
   through a queue or staff space.

## Implementation and acceptance

- Carry forward the current assembly and repaired routes. Do not revive retired
  modules from older plans while adding details.
- Register each proposed detail area against current plots, public/service
  circulation, protected areas and views. Use the strict WorldSpec/greybox
  workflow for any addition large enough to form a new public module.
- For small attachments, first produce a footprint/clearance preview on the
  owning module. Record purpose, support, guest access, return/service access,
  review view and block allowance. Review that preview before detailing it.
- Preserve every existing paved cell and route width. No carpet, planter, post
  or decorative block goes on the walking surface. Overhead additions maintain
  generous clearance; the existing bunting's six clear courses is a useful
  local standard.
- Target zero excavation. If an isolated mounting/fixture replacement is useful,
  list its exact old/new blocks separately; do not hide removals in an add pack.
- Produce an **addition-only schematic** and a full comparison preview, so the
  work can be built without reprinting the park. Any small replacements get
  their own state-checked list.
- Run collision, legal-material, support, route and lamp checks; render the same
  guest views before/after. Then check the first built section in Minecraft by
  day and night before repeating its details elsewhere.
- A first-time visitor should be able to identify the next destination, find
  the entrance, stand aside to read, and find a return without an explanation
  from the builder. Test that with another player when the section is placed.
